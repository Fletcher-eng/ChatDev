# 3.2 Causes, consequences and solutions

Part of [[GP 3 Issues and perspectives]] in [[Global Perspectives]]. Written by the Specpoint team and based on the Cambridge 0457 skills.

**[[Cause|Causes]] → [[Issue]] → [[Consequence|Consequences]] → [[Course of action]]**

- A [[Cause]] is a reason why an issue happens.
- A [[Consequence]] is a result or effect of an issue.
- A [[Course of action]] is a possible way to tackle the issue.

### Finding causes and consequences: PESTLE

Many teachers use [[PESTLE]] to make sure you think widely: **P**olitical, **E**conomic, **S**ocial, **T**echnological, **L**egal, **E**nvironmental. For example, a consequence of plastic pollution could be economic (fewer tourists) or environmental (harm to sea life).

### Judging a course of action

| Ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Will it work? | Does it tackle the cause, or just the symptoms? |
| How much will it cost? | Can people or governments afford it? |
| Who gains and who loses? | Different stakeholders are affected differently. |
| How long will it take? | Some fixes are quick, others take years. |
| Any side effects? | A solution can cause new problems. |

> [!warning] Common mistake
> Suggesting a course of action without explaining **how** it would solve the issue. Always link the solution to a cause.

> [!question] Quick check
> A possible way to tackle an issue is called a…
>
> A. Consequence
> B. Cause
> C. Perspective
> D. Course of action
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **Course of action**. A course of action is a proposed solution.
