# 1.1 SEEEL paragraphs

Part of [[GP 1 Writing arguments]] in [[Global Perspectives]]. Written by the Specpoint team and based on the Cambridge 0457 skills.

In Global Perspectives you are marked on how well you build an argument, not on how many facts you know. [[SEEEL]] gives every paragraph the same strong shape, so the examiner can see each skill.

**Statement → [[Evidence]] → Explain → Evaluate → Link**

| Letter | Stands for | What you write | Sentence starter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S** | Statement | One clear point that answers the question. | "One reason why … is that …" |
| **E** | [[Evidence]] | A fact, statistic, example or quote that backs it up. Say where it comes from. | "According to …" |
| **E** | Explain | How the evidence proves your point. | "This shows that … because …" |
| **E** | Evaluate | How strong the evidence is, or what another perspective would say. | "However, this evidence may be limited because …" |
| **L** | Link | Bring it back to the question or your overall argument. | "Therefore …" |

> [!example] Example SEEEL paragraph
> **Question:** Should governments ban single use plastic bags?
> 
> 1. **Statement:** One reason governments should ban single use plastic bags is that they harm wildlife in the oceans.
> 2. **Evidence:** The United Nations Environment Programme reports that plastic pollution is a serious threat to marine animals, which can swallow it or get tangled in it.
> 3. **Explain:** This shows that plastic bags are not just untidy. They damage ecosystems that many communities rely on for food and jobs.
> 4. **Evaluate:** However, the report covers all plastic waste, not only bags, so a ban on bags alone may have a smaller effect than people expect.
> 5. **Link:** Therefore, a ban is a useful first step, but it works best alongside wider action on plastic.

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> **Evaluate** is the E most students skip, and it is what lifts an answer into the top marks. Always ask yourself how far you can trust your evidence.

> [!warning] Common mistake
> Repeating the evidence in different words and calling it an explanation. **Explain** must say *how* or *why* the evidence proves your point.

Some teachers use [[PEEL]] (Point, Evidence, Explain, Link). SEEEL is the same idea with an extra E for Evaluate. If your teacher uses different words, edit your flashcards to match.

> [!question] Quick check
> Which part of a SEEEL paragraph judges how strong the evidence is?
>
> A. Explain
> B. Evidence
> C. Link
> D. Evaluate
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **Evaluate**. Explain says how the evidence supports your point. Evaluate judges how strong or reliable it is.
