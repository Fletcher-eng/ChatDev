# 4.1 Research

Part of [[GP 4 Research and assessment]] in [[Global Perspectives]]. Written by the Specpoint team and based on the Cambridge 0457 skills.

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Primary research]] | Information you collect yourself | Surveys, questionnaires, interviews, observations, experiments |
| [[Secondary research]] | Information someone else has already collected | Books, articles, reports, websites, documentaries |
| [[Quantitative data]] | Data in numbers | "68% of students walk to school" |
| [[Qualitative data]] | Data in words, views and descriptions | "I walk because the bus is unreliable" |

- A [[Research question]] guides what you look for. Good ones are focused and open, for example "To what extent should my school ban single use plastic?"
- Your [[Sample]] is the group of people you survey. A bigger and more varied sample gives more reliable results.
- Avoid leading questions such as "Don't you agree plastic is terrible?" because they push people towards an answer.

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> Say what your research **showed** and how it shaped your view. Listing what you did without saying what you learned scores less.

> [!question] Quick check
> A questionnaire you write and hand out yourself is…
>
> A. Secondary research
> B. A course of action
> C. A value judgement
> D. Primary research
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **Primary research**. Primary research is information you collect yourself.
