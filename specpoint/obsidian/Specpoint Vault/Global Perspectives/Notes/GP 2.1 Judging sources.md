# 2.1 Judging sources

Part of [[GP 2 Sources and evidence]] in [[Global Perspectives]]. Written by the Specpoint team and based on the Cambridge 0457 skills.

In GP you must decide how far you can trust each source. Two words matter most.

- [[Credibility]]: whether the *author or source* is believable. Think about who made it and why.
- [[Reliability]]: whether the *information* can be trusted. Think about the evidence it uses and whether other sources agree.

### What to check

| Factor | Question to ask | More trustworthy if… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Expertise]] | Is the author qualified to write about this? | They are a scientist, researcher or professional in the field |
| [[Vested interest]] | Does the author gain if people believe this? | They have nothing to gain or sell |
| [[Bias]] | Does it only show one side? | It presents more than one view fairly |
| [[Evidence]] | Does it back up its claims? | It gives data, examples and references |
| Date | Is it up to date? | It is recent, or the facts have not changed |
| Purpose | Is it trying to inform, persuade or sell? | It aims to inform |
| Publisher | Where was it published? | A respected organisation, journal or news outlet |

Many schools teach the [[CRAAP test]] as a quick checklist: **C**urrency (how up to date), **R**elevance (does it answer your question), **A**uthority (who wrote it), **A**ccuracy (is it backed by evidence), **P**urpose (why it was written).

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> Always give a specific reason. "The author is a climate scientist at a university, so they have expertise" scores. "It looks reliable" does not.

> [!warning] Common mistake
> Saying a source is unreliable just because it is on the internet or ends in.com. Judge the author, evidence and purpose instead.

> [!question] Quick check
> A website that sells electric cars says electric cars are the best way to cut pollution. What is the main problem?
>
> A. It is too old
> B. It uses statistics
> C. It is a primary source
> D. It has a vested interest
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **It has a vested interest**. The site gains if you believe it, so it may leave out drawbacks.
