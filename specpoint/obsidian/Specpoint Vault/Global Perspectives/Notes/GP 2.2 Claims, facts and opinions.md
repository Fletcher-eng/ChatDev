# 2.2 Claims, facts and opinions

Part of [[GP 2 Sources and evidence]] in [[Global Perspectives]]. Written by the Specpoint team and based on the Cambridge 0457 skills.

Arguments are built from [[Claim|claims]]. Your job is to spot what kind of claim each one is and how well it is supported.

| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Fact]] | Can be checked and proved true or false | "Plastic takes hundreds of years to break down." |
| [[Opinion]] | A personal belief or view | "Plastic bags are annoying." |
| [[Value judgement]] | An opinion about what is good, bad, right or wrong | "It is wrong to use plastic when alternatives exist." |
| [[Prediction]] | A claim about what will or may happen | "Plastic waste in the oceans will keep increasing unless we act." |

- [[Evidence]] is the information used to support a claim, such as data, examples or expert views.
- [[Reasoning]] is how the evidence is linked to the conclusion. Good reasoning is logical and does not jump to conclusions.

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> Words like **should**, **must**, **fair**, **wrong**, **best** usually signal a value judgement. Words like **will** and **by 2050** signal a prediction.

> [!question] Quick check
> "Governments should tax sugary drinks" is…
>
> A. A fact
> B. A prediction
> C. A statistic
> D. A value judgement
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **A value judgement**. The word should shows a judgement about what is right.
