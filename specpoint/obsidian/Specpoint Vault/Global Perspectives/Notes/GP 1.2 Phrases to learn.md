# 1.2 Phrases to learn

Part of [[GP 1 Writing arguments]] in [[Global Perspectives]]. Written by the Specpoint team and based on the Cambridge 0457 skills.

Strong answers use the same small set of phrases again and again. Learn two from each group really well rather than all of them badly.

| Job | Phrases |
| --- | --- |
| Making a point | "One reason why …" · "The main argument for … is …" · "A key cause of … is …" |
| Using evidence | "According to …" · "For example, …" · "Research by … found that …" |
| Explaining | "This shows that …" · "This means that …" · "As a result, …" |
| Evaluating | "However, …" · "This source may be biased because …" · "This evidence is limited because …" |
| Other perspectives | "On the other hand, …" · "From a national perspective, …" · "Some people argue that …" |
| Linking and concluding | "Therefore, …" · "Overall, …" · "This suggests that …" |

### Arguments and conclusions

- An [[Argument]] is a set of reasons that lead to a conclusion.
- A [[Counter argument]] is a reason against your view. Mentioning it, then answering it, makes your own argument stronger.
- A [[Conclusion]] is the judgement you reach at the end. It should follow from your reasons, not appear from nowhere.

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> When a question asks for your view, give a clear judgement in the conclusion. Sitting on the fence loses marks unless you explain exactly why the answer depends on something.

> [!question] Quick check
> "On the other hand, some people argue that …" is a phrase for…
>
> A. Giving evidence
> B. Linking back to the question
> C. Making your first point
> D. Bringing in another perspective
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **Bringing in another perspective**. It introduces a different point of view, which is part of evaluating an argument.
