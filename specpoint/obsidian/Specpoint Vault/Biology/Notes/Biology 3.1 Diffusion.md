# 3.1 Diffusion

Part of [[Biology 3 Movement into and out of cells]] in [[Biology]]. Written by the Specpoint team and checked against the Cambridge 0610 syllabus.

[[Diffusion]] is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient, as a result of their random movement.

The energy for diffusion comes from the ==kinetic energy of random movement== of molecules and ions. The cell does not use energy from respiration, so diffusion is a passive process.

Substances move into and out of cells by diffusion through the cell membrane. Examples include oxygen and carbon dioxide during gas exchange, and dissolved substances (solutes) moving between cells and the fluid around them.

### What changes the rate of diffusion

| Factor | Faster diffusion when… | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Surface area]] | Larger | More space for particles to cross at once |
| Temperature | Higher | Particles have more kinetic energy and move faster |
| [[Concentration gradient]] | Steeper | Bigger difference means more net movement |
| Distance | Shorter | Particles have less far to travel |

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> Always write **net movement** in your definition. Particles move both ways, but more move from high to low concentration.

> [!question] Quick check
> Which change would SLOW diffusion down?
>
> A. A higher temperature
> B. A larger surface area
> C. A steeper concentration gradient
> D. A longer diffusion distance
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **A longer diffusion distance**. Particles have further to travel, so diffusion takes longer.
