# 2.2 Size of specimens

Part of [[Biology 2 Organisation of the organism]] in [[Biology]]. Written by the Specpoint team and checked against the Cambridge 0610 syllabus.

Microscopes make cells look bigger. To work out how much bigger, you use the magnification formula. This is one of the most reliable calculation marks on Paper 4.

> [!info] Formula
> magnification = image size ÷ actual size
> actual size = image size ÷ magnification
> image size = actual size × magnification

Magnification has ==no units==. Write it with a times sign, for example **×400**.

### Millimetres and micrometres **(Supplement)**

Cells are tiny, so their actual size is often given in micrometres (µm).

> [!info] Formula
> 1 mm = 1000 µm
> mm to µm: multiply by 1000
> µm to mm: divide by 1000

Before you divide, ==both sizes must be in the same unit==.

> [!example] Worked example 1
> A drawing of a cheek cell is 45 mm wide. The real cell is 60 µm wide. Calculate the magnification.
> 
> 1. Convert to the same unit: 60 µm ÷ 1000 = 0.06 mm
> 2. Image size ÷ actual size: 45 ÷ 0.06 = 750
> 3. Answer: **×750**

> [!example] Worked example 2
> An image of a cell is 30 mm long at ×400. Find its actual length in µm.
> 
> 1. Actual size = 30 ÷ 400 = 0.075 mm
> 2. Convert: 0.075 × 1000 = **75 µm**

> [!example] Interactive on Specpoint
> Magnification calculator. Open this topic on the Specpoint website to use it.

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> Measure image sizes with a ruler in millimetres, and check what unit the question wants your answer in.

> [!warning] Common mistake
> Dividing actual size by image size. The image is the big number, so it goes on top. If your magnification is less than 1, you have it upside down.

> [!question] Quick check
> An image is 20 mm long. The real object is 0.5 mm long. What is the magnification?
>
> A. ×10
> B. ×0.025
> C. ×100
> D. ×40
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **×40**. Image size ÷ actual size: 20 ÷ 0.5 = ×40.
