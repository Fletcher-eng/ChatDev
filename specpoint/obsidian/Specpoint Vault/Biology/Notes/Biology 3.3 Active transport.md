# 3.3 Active transport

Part of [[Biology 3 Movement into and out of cells]] in [[Biology]]. Written by the Specpoint team and checked against the Cambridge 0610 syllabus.

[[Active transport]] is the movement of particles through a cell membrane from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration, using energy from respiration. It moves substances **against** a concentration gradient.

Example: root hair cells take in mineral ions from the soil by active transport, because the soil has a lower concentration of ions than the cell.

**(Supplement)** [[Protein carriers]] in the membrane move the molecules or ions across during active transport.

### Comparing the three processes

|  | [[Diffusion]] | [[Osmosis]] | [[Active transport]] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What moves | Any particles | Water only | Molecules or ions |
| Direction | Down the gradient | Higher to lower water potential | Against the gradient |
| Energy from respiration | No | No | Yes |
| Needs a membrane | No | Yes, partially permeable | Yes, with protein carriers |

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> If a question mentions a cell with lots of mitochondria, think active transport. More mitochondria means more respiration to supply the energy.

> [!question] Quick check
> Why do cells that carry out lots of active transport have many mitochondria?
>
> A. To make more protein carriers
> B. To store water
> C. To absorb light
> D. To release more energy from respiration
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **To release more energy from respiration**. Active transport needs energy from respiration, and respiration happens in mitochondria.
