# 3.2 Osmosis

Part of [[Biology 3 Movement into and out of cells]] in [[Biology]]. Written by the Specpoint team and checked against the Cambridge 0610 syllabus.

Water diffuses through partially permeable membranes by [[Osmosis]]. Water moves into and out of cells by osmosis through the cell membrane.

**(Supplement)** The full definition: osmosis is the net movement of water molecules from a region of [[Water potential|higher water potential]] (a dilute solution) to a region of [[Water potential|lower water potential]] (a concentrated solution), through a partially permeable membrane.

### Plant cells **(Supplement)**

- In a dilute solution, water enters by osmosis. The cell becomes [[Turgid]]. Water pushing outwards on the cell wall creates [[Turgor pressure]], which supports the plant.
- In a concentrated solution, water leaves by osmosis. The cell becomes [[Flaccid]]. If it loses a lot of water, the membrane pulls away from the cell wall. This is [[Plasmolysis]].
- A plant wilts when its cells become flaccid and lose turgor pressure.

### Animal cells **(Supplement)**

Animal cells have no cell wall. In a dilute solution they gain water and can ==burst==. In a concentrated solution they lose water and ==shrink==. This is why the water potential of blood plasma must be kept steady.

### The potato practical

Cylinders of potato are weighed, left in sucrose solutions of different concentrations, then blotted dry and weighed again.

> [!info] Formula
> percentage change = (change in mass ÷ starting mass) × 100

A gain in mass means water entered. A loss means water left. Where the mass does not change, the water potential of the potato equals that of the solution.

> [!warning] Common mistake
> Saying "water moves from high concentration to low concentration". That describes the solute, not water. Talk about **water potential**, or about moving from a dilute to a concentrated solution.

> [!question] Quick check
> Which word describes a plant cell full of water, pressing on its cell wall?
>
> A. Flaccid
> B. Plasmolysed
> C. Burst
> D. Turgid
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **Turgid**. Turgid cells push outwards on the wall. That turgor pressure supports the plant.
