# 2.1 Cell structure

Part of [[Biology 2 Organisation of the organism]] in [[Biology]]. Written by the Specpoint team and checked against the Cambridge 0610 syllabus.

All living organisms are made of cells. ==New cells are produced by division of existing cells.== Animal, plant and bacterial cells share some structures and differ in others, and the exam asks you to compare them.

### Explore the cells

> [!example] Interactive on Specpoint
> Cell explorer: tap each part of an animal, plant and bacterial cell. Open this topic on the Specpoint website to use it.

### Structures and what they do

| Structure | Function | Animal | Plant | [[Bacterial cell\|Bacterium]] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[Cell membrane]] | Controls what enters and leaves the cell. It is partially permeable. | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| [[Cytoplasm]] | Where most chemical reactions in the cell take place. | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| [[Ribosomes]] | Protein synthesis. | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| [[Nucleus]] | Contains the genetic material (chromosomes) that controls the cell. | Yes | Yes | No |
| [[Mitochondria]] | Aerobic respiration, which releases energy. | Yes | Yes | No |
| [[Cell wall]] | Strengthens the cell and keeps its shape. Made of cellulose in plants. | No | Yes | Yes |
| [[Chloroplasts]] | Absorb light energy for photosynthesis. | No | Yes | No |
| [[Large permanent vacuole]] | Contains cell sap. Helps keep the cell firm. | No | Yes | No |
| Circular DNA and plasmids | Genetic material of a bacterium. | No | No | Yes |

> [!tip] Examiner tip
> Plant cells have three structures that animal cells lack: a **cell wall**, **chloroplasts** and a **large permanent vacuole**. When a question asks you to compare them, name all three. Bacteria have a cell wall too, but it is not made of cellulose.

> [!warning] Common mistake
> Saying the cell wall controls what enters the cell. The cell wall is fully permeable. Only the **cell membrane** controls entry and exit.

### Specialised cells

Cells become [[Specialised cell|specialised]] so they can carry out one job well. Learn the function of each cell below and one way its structure helps.

| Cell | Function | How it is adapted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Ciliated cell]] | Moves mucus in the trachea and bronchi | Cilia beat to sweep mucus, with trapped dust and pathogens, away from the lungs |
| [[Root hair cell]] | Absorbs water and mineral ions | Long extension gives a large surface area |
| [[Palisade mesophyll cell]] | [[Photosynthesis]] | Packed with chloroplasts |
| [[Neurone]] | Conducts electrical impulses | Long fibre carries impulses over long distances |
| [[Red blood cell]] | Transports oxygen | Contains haemoglobin and has no nucleus, leaving more room for it |
| [[Sperm and egg cells]] | Reproduction | Sperm have a flagellum to swim. Egg cells have a large food store in the cytoplasm |

### Levels of organisation

**Cell → [[Tissue]] → [[Organ]] → [[Organ system]] → Organism**

- [[Tissue]]: a group of cells with similar structures, working together to perform a shared function. Example: muscle tissue.
- [[Organ]]: a structure made of a group of tissues, working together to perform specific functions. Example: the heart.
- [[Organ system]]: a group of organs with related functions, working together to perform body functions. Example: the circulatory system.

> [!question] Quick check
> Which structure do plant cells share with bacteria but NOT with animal cells?
>
> A. Chloroplasts
> B. Nucleus
> C. Large permanent vacuole
> D. Cell wall
>
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **Cell wall**. Bacteria have a cell wall too, but it is not made of cellulose.
